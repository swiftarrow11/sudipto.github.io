# The Trouble with Too Many Devices

Today I want to talk about a problem that I have been facing for a while now. It’s something that many of you might relate to, especially if you are a tech enthusiast like me. The problem is: I have too many personal devices and I don’t know how to use them all and when to use which one.

Let me explain. Over the years, I have accumulated a number of gadgets that are supposed to make my life easier and more enjoyable. I have an iPhone, an iPad, a Kindle, and a laptop. Each one of these devices has its own purpose and function, but somehow, they also overlap and compete with each other.

For example, my iPhone is my go-to device when I’m on the move. It’s always in my pocket or in my hand, ready to make calls, send texts, check emails, browse social media, take photos, play games, listen to music, watch videos…you name it. It’s basically an extension of myself.

But then there’s my iPad. It’s bigger and better than my iPhone in many ways. It has a larger screen that makes reading and watching more comfortable and immersive. It has more storage space that allows me to download more apps and media files. It has better speakers that produce richer sound quality. I even have 5G enabled on it for on the go usage.

So why don’t I use it more often? Well…because it’s too big and heavy to carry around everywhere. And because most of the things I can do on my iPad, I can also do on my phone (albeit with less quality). So, the iPad ends up sitting on my couch or on my bedside table or happily in my bagpack most of the time.

Then there’s my Kindle. It’s the best device for reading books (especially novels) hands down. It has an e-ink screen that mimics paper and doesn’t strain the eyes even after hours of reading. It has a long battery life that lasts for weeks without charging. It has access to millions of books from Amazon.

So why don’t I read more books? Well…because sometimes I get distracted by other things (like my phone or my iPad). And because sometimes I prefer reading magazines or articles instead of books (which are better suited for the iPad). So, it is to say other devices come in the way.

Finally, there’s my laptop. It’s the most powerful and versatile device among all four. It can do everything that the other devices can do (and more). It has a full keyboard that makes typing easier and faster. It has a large monitor that displays 4k graphics and videos. It has multiple ports that connect to various peripherals and accessories.

So why don’t I use it all the time? Well…because it’s relatively bulky to use anywhere other than at home or at work (and sometimes not even there). And because sometimes it feels like overkill to use such a sophisticated machine for simple tasks (like checking email or watching YouTube). So, the laptop ends up being shut down or in sleep mode most of the time.

As you can see, having too many devices is not as fun as it sounds. Instead of making me more productive and entertained, they make me more confused and frustrated. They create clutter in my space and in my mind.

So, what should I do? Should I get rid of some of them? Should I try to use them more equally? Should I buy new ones that are better integrated with each other?

I don’t know yet…but maybe you do! If you have any advice or suggestions on how to deal with this problem (or if you have this problem yourself), please leave a comment below!

Thank you for reading this post! Until next time!
